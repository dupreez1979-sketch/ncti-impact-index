About the Index

The Index scores every community in Australia for the social value that children's theatre can create there — a single number from 0 to 10, so touring and subsidy can be directed to where they matter most.

The three measures

The detail
1 · What the Index is▾

The Index answers a practical question for the National Children's Theatre Initiative: with limited touring and subsidy budgets, where should children's theatre go first to reach the children who stand to benefit most?

Every community in the country is scored from 0 to 10 — higher means a greater opportunity for impact. The guiding vision is simple: Theatre for Every Child. The Index turns that ambition into evidence, so touring routes and subsidy can be directed transparently rather than by habit or proximity.

2 · How the score is calculated▾

The Index blends three measures, each converted to its own 0–10 sub-score, then averaged using weights you control:

  • Emotional maturity — the local rate of children developmentally vulnerable or at-risk on the AEDC emotional-maturity domain, scaled against the national average (~25%). An average area scores 5; much higher rates approach 10.
  • Disadvantage — the area's SEIFA decile, flipped so the most disadvantaged 10% of areas score 10 and the least disadvantaged score 1.
  • Remoteness — 0 for major cities, rising through 2.5 (inner regional), 5 (outer regional) and 7.5 (remote) to 10 (very remote).

The default weighting is 40% emotional maturity, 40% disadvantage, 20% remoteness. Every weight is adjustable, and any indicator can be switched off — the remaining weights rebalance to 100%.

3 · The three indicators — and why▾

Emotional maturity (child wellbeing). Children's theatre's clearest social contribution is to emotional and social development. The AEDC emotional-maturity domain is the best national, place-based measure of where young children are struggling, so it sits at the heart of the Index.

Disadvantage (access). Families in disadvantaged areas have the least access to professional live performance — cost, distance and fewer local venues. SEIFA points the Index toward communities the market underserves.

Remoteness (isolation). Remote communities have the thinnest cultural infrastructure of all; remoteness lifts their score to reflect the extra value of reaching them.

Why we sometimes remove remoteness. Remoteness and disadvantage often move together, so counting both can double-weight the same places. Turning remoteness off — a 50/50 emotional-maturity and disadvantage model — isolates developmental need and disadvantage on their own terms. The sandbox lets you compare both views.

4 · Geography: SA2 and SA3▾

Every figure sits on the ABS Australian Statistical Geography Standard, at two levels:

  • SA2 — roughly a suburb or town (~10,000 people): the local community around a venue.
  • SA3 — a cluster of SA2s, closer to the touring catchment a venue realistically draws from.

The geography toggle switches the whole model between the two, so you can score the precise local area or the broader catchment.

5 · Tiers and the subsidy model▾

Scores band into three priority tiers — High, Medium, Low — at thresholds you set (default High ≥ 6.3, Medium ≥ 3.8).

Subsidy applies to High-tier areas only, and ramps up within the band: from a floor (default 50%) at the High threshold to full subsidy (100%) near the top of the scale (default index 8.0+), in 5% steps — so the highest-impact places attract the deepest support.

When you cost a package of venues, the total subsidy can be split across funders: the home state government covers its own state's venues, Playing Australia covers the rest of the country, and philanthropy can optionally take a share of that remainder.

6 · The modelling sandbox and saved models▾

There is no single "correct" weighting — different funders and purposes justify different emphases. The dashboard is a sandbox: change the weights, geography, thresholds and subsidy shape, and every area and venue re-scores instantly.

This keeps the model transparent and lets you stress-test how sensitive the priorities are to each choice. Useful configurations can be saved as named models (with a description and date) so agreed scenarios can be compared side by side.

7 · Where the data comes from▾

Everything is drawn from official, public sources at the SA2/SA3 level:

  • Emotional maturity — Australian Early Development Census (AEDC) 2024, emotional-maturity domain.
  • Disadvantage — ABS SEIFA 2021, Index of Relative Socio-economic Disadvantage (IRSD).
  • Remoteness — ABS Remoteness Areas (ASGS 2021).
  • Children in the area — ABS Estimated Resident Population by age (0–4, 5–9, 10–14).
  • Community profile (in "About the area") — ABS Census 2021: income, rent, employment, family type, education, First Nations population, language and housing.

Child population is used as context — the reach, or number of children a venue could serve — not as part of the score. The Index measures the intensity of opportunity; population tells you how many children it touches.

8 · How it is used and deployed▾

The Index supports three decisions: where to tour, where to subsidise, and how to make the case to funders.

Venues are mapped against the national picture so gaps — high-impact communities with no current venue — are visible. The subsidy basket lets you select venues, apply an assumed fee, and see the total investment and funding split.

The tool is a live web application: the data lives in a database, so venues, saved models and scenarios can be edited in place and shared by link, and the figures can be refreshed as new ABS and AEDC releases arrive.

Sources: AEDC 2024 · ABS SEIFA 2021 (IRSD) · ABS Remoteness Areas (ASGS 2021) · ABS Estimated Resident Population · ABS Census 2021. Built for the National Children's Theatre Initiative.
